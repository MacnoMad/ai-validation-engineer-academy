Next steps

Two separate tracks from here, and you don't have to choose just one.

Track A — Becoming a working AI validation engineer (do this regardless of the exam)

Build a portfolio artifact

Pick a small, real open-source ML model (a Hugging Face text classifier, an image model, anything with a public model card) and write a genuine test plan for it: input data risks, performance metrics with thresholds, a few metamorphic relations, an adversarial probe or two, and a documented pass/fail verdict. This is the single most convincing thing you can show a hiring manager — more convincing than any certificate.

Contribute to AI-testing tooling

Open-source evaluation and observability projects (LLM eval harnesses, dataset-quality checkers, drift-monitoring libraries) almost always need test cases, docs fixes, or small feature contributions. Even a handful of merged pull requests demonstrates you can operate in this space for real.

Stay current deliberately

This field moves fast — new model architectures, new evaluation techniques, new regulation. Follow AI safety and AI-testing communities and practitioner blogs, skim model cards for major new releases, and revisit the labs in Sessions 17–20 periodically; the concepts age well even as specific tools change.

Know where this skillset lands

Everything in this course applies directly to QA/test roles at companies shipping AI features, data-quality roles (Sessions 14–16 especially), ML/MLOps-adjacent engineering roles that need someone rigorous about evaluation, and product or delivery roles that need to reason about AI risk. This is a genuinely transferable skillset, not a single job title.

Track B — If you want the official ISTQB CT-AI v2.0 credential

🎓
Informational only — not a registration step

The Certified Tester AI Testing (CT-AI) exam requires holding the ISTQB Foundation Level (CTFL) certificate first — that's the entry requirement, so confirm you have it before planning around this.

Beyond that, exam delivery is handled by accredited national or regional ISTQB member boards and their exam providers, not by ISTQB centrally — check istqb.org for the member board covering your region to find an accredited provider, current pricing, and available sitting formats (online proctored vs. in-person both exist in most regions).

This training deliberately covers the same 40-question, 60-minute, 65%-pass structure so the mechanics of Part 1 above feel familiar if you do sit it — but pursuing the credential itself is entirely optional and up to you.
